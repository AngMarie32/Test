The 2026 Prop Firm Trader Tax Checklist & Expense Deduction Guide

Forms to expect, expenses to track, records to keep, and 2026 estimated tax dates. Check items off as you go, or print it.

1. Start with the payout arrangement

Before anything else, figure out how you were paid. A payout from a funded futures account is not automatically your own Section 1256 capital gain. For a typical sole-proprietor independent-contractor arrangement, net payouts generally go on Schedule C with self-employment tax on Schedule SE. Employment, entity and other arrangements may differ, and the 1099 label alone is not decisive.

  • Download your trader agreement from every firm
  • Note whether you contracted as an individual or through an LLC / S-Corp

2. Forms to expect

  • Collect every form above, from every firm and broker
  • Export your full payout history from each firm, including any that sent no form
  • Ask the issuer to correct any form that doesn't match what you received

3. Expenses to track

Deduct only ordinary and necessary business expenses with records behind them. Evaluation and reset fees may be current expenses or startup costs depending on when and why you paid them. Not every fee is automatically deductible.

  • Evaluation, challenge, reset and activation fees (with dates)
  • Market data and exchange fees
  • Platform and routing subscriptions (NinjaTrader, Tradovate, Rithmic, and similar)
  • Charting and analytics (TradingView and similar)
  • VPS / cloud servers used for trading
  • Computers, monitors and equipment used for the business (keep purchase dates)
  • Business share of internet and phone
  • Trading education that maintains or improves skills in an existing trading business (courses taken before you started generally don't qualify)
  • Home office, only if the space is used regularly and exclusively for the business
  • Tax preparation and bookkeeping fees for the business

4. Records to keep

  • Trader agreements and any amendments
  • Payout history per firm (dates, gross amounts, fees, net)
  • Receipts for every expense above, with dates
  • Broker statements and year-end tax packages
  • Records of estimated tax payments (date, amount, confirmation number)
  • For own-account traders: trade logs and time spent, if you may claim Trader Tax Status

5. 2026 estimated tax dates

Prop firms withhold nothing. If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, federal estimates are generally due:

PeriodDue date
Q1 2026April 15, 2026
Q2 2026June 15, 2026
Q3 2026September 15, 2026
Q4 2026January 15, 2027

To generally avoid an underpayment penalty, pay at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000). If your payouts came late in the year, the annualized income method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) may lower your earlier installments. Check your state's estimated tax dates too.

6. If you also trade your own account

  • Futures and other Section 1256 contracts: 60/40 treatment and year-end mark-to-market on Form 6781
  • Stocks and options: reconcile wash sales across all your accounts, including IRAs
  • Thinking about Trader Tax Status or the Section 475(f) election? For existing filers the election is generally due by April 15 for that tax year
  • Forex: spot forex is generally Section 988 ordinary income or loss unless a valid election was made before the trade
